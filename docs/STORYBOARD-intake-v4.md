# Intake, the top object: THE CATCH-ALL (storyboard, 2026-10-21, branch intake-v4, fresh eyes)

Jon after the pile: "The view of the calendar isn't good enough to be relevant. I don't know why we need the phone to
be on the right side as well. There's too many other notifications with extra words that feel irrelevant and hard to
navigate through. And the subtext saying tap what's yours is stupid and weird." And before that, what he liked: the
lived frame (the phone as it was, sorted into Intake), one press at a time, the calendar beneath that changes, the memo
whose words arrive as spoken.

The argument in one line: "the two things I already use, voice memos and iMessage, sorted into tickets. My calendar,
so many iOS-based things, in a sorted feed. All of my notifications are turned off." A catch-all.

The lived moment: you pick up your phone. Five things you sent yourself are waiting on the lock screen, in five
shapes: a photo of a save-the-date, a screenshot of a concert email, a Marketplace link with a thought, a memo from
your wrist, two words typed at 1:41 AM. Nothing else: his notifications are off. Beside the phone, INTAKE,
an empty outline of it (round two). Beside that, his calendar, a real month, October 2026, today ringed. Each thing is one
press: it opens so you see what it is, then it goes into Intake and becomes its ticket; the dated ones also land on
the calendar, which pages to the day. After the fifth the lock screen is the clock alone.

Where is Intake: its outline on screen from the first paint (the middle column, the phone's silhouette as one
hairline on the paper), its cards at every landing, and the whole end state. Round two (2026-10-21, Jon: "I don't
know why it needs to look like a phone or why there needs to be header info there and the dark black background.
What matters is showing the cards"): no app chrome, only the cards. Apple appears only as the lock screen the things arrive on, and the calendar
they land in.

## The fold under the watch (round seven, 2026-10-22: THE SPINE)

Jon's thesis: "a catch-all system that doesn't care about the location or medium of how the information is getting
into the system"; of the old hook: "I don't think it's my best idea that my calendar changed." The sheet's three jobs:
the catch-all proves any way in; the watch proves right now, you (`try it`); the fold proves WHAT COMES OUT, one breath,
five changes, against what that would have cost by hand.

- AT REST: the hook alone, `It doesn't care how it got in.` (DRAFT, WATCH_COPY.hook in objects-copy.ts; the locked
  headline stays in builds-copy.ts for the case study), and the chevron.
- OPEN, in order: THE SENTENCE once, short (DRAFT, intake-run.json → proof.said), the accent quote marks; then TWO
  MODULES side by side (stacked under 820), one word over each in the label face: BY HAND = what the sentence costs in
  the Calendar app, a pile of four New Event sheets in iOS's grammar in the sheet's dim ink (Title · Location · All-day ·
  Starts · Ends · Repeat · Alert, the four titles and the real times), the Delete Event confirmation for the wedding on
  top, its red the only colour, static; INTAKE = Fri 5 · Sat 6 · Sun 7 of March 2027 in the catch-all's calendar
  vocabulary; the click that opens the fold is the press: the wedding bar leaves (400 ms), a 600 ms hold, then Dinner
  6 PM, Yoga 9 AM, Sam's party 8 PM, Thrift run 1 PM arrive 220 ms apart; closing resets it; reduced motion shows the
  after state. Nothing else: no Siri (junkyard/IntakeSiri.astro), no fact line, no whole story on this sheet.

## The haters

- "Five presses? Nobody clicks five times." → One press already shows the whole idea in two seconds: the thing opens, goes in, comes out a ticket, and the calendar turns to the day. The rims say what is left; the memo is the one worth waiting for.
- "A month grid under two columns is a lot of object." → It is the one view everyone can read at a glance, and it is the after: the calendar he no longer opens, filling itself. A strip of numerals was not it; this is drawn the way his phone draws it.
- "Where is the noise, if his notifications are off?" → The noise is the five shapes: a photo, a screenshot, a link, a memo, two words at 1:41 AM, all in the same grey glass, nothing done. The after is the same five sorted into kinds, two of them already done. Junk with words only got in the way.
